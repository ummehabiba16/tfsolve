**Rational?** Yes. Rationality means choosing the action that maximises expected performance given the percepts. When the hand touches a hot stove, every fraction of a second of contact causes more damage. A reflex pulls the hand away before the signal even reaches the brain; deliberating ("this is hot, it will burn me, I should move my hand") would be slower and lead to a worse outcome. So the reflex is the best action available: it is rational. AIMA notes that acting rationally does not require inference; reflexes are a case where acting without deliberation is better.

**Intelligent?** It depends on the definition:

- They are **not intelligent** in the sense of thought or reasoning: the action is not the result of the agent understanding or deciding anything, and the agent cannot adapt the reflex to circumstances (it would also flinch from a stove that is safe).

- They **are intelligent behaviour** in the sense that they are the product of an intelligent "design" process: evolution selected them because they work. The intelligence lies in the design, not in the moment of acting.

So: rational, yes; intelligent as an act of the individual, no, but they are an example of intelligent (rational) behaviour that does not require thinking.
