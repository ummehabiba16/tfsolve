The Opportunity rover, which operated on Mars from 2004 to 2018, faced challenges such as power supply issues, terrain navigation, communication reliability, mechanical wear, and environmental hazards like dust storms. A Bayesian network with five random variables can represent the probabilistic relationships among these factors to model the rover's operational uncertainty. We select the following random variables:

**Power Supply (P):** Represents the rover's sufficient power status (Yes, No), primarily dependent on solar panel efficiency, which can be affected by dust accumulation.

**Dust Storm (D):** Represents the occurrence of a dust storm (Yes, No), which impacts solar power generation.

**Terrain Difficulty (T):** Represents the terrain's navigability (Yes, No), affecting the rover's mobility and risk of getting stuck.

**Mobility (M):** Represents the rover's ability to move properly (Yes, No), influenced by terrain and mechanical condition.

**Communication (C):** Represents the rover's ability communicate with Earth reliably (Yes, No), dependent on power and environmental conditions.

The following Bayesian Network with conditional probability table is given (Figure for Question 5(a)) to model the Opportunity rover's uncertain Martian environment.

![Figure for Question 5(a)](figures/q5-1.png)

| Variable | Parents | CPT |
|:--|:--|:--|
| D | none | $P(D) = 0.2$ |
| T | none | $P(T) = 0.5$ |
| P | D | $P(P \mid D{=}\text{Yes}) = 0.4$, $P(P \mid D{=}\text{No}) = 0.9$ |
| C | P | $P(C \mid P{=}\text{Yes}) = 0.9$, $P(C \mid P{=}\text{No}) = 0.3$ |
| M | T, P | Yes,Yes: 0.95; Yes,No: 0.4; No,Yes: 0.6; No,No: 0.1 |

*The table repeats the figure's CPTs; for M the pairs are (T, P).*
