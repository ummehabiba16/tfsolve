**Is the latter statement true?** Essentially **yes**, at the physical level. Everything in brains or computers is made of atoms that obey the laws of physics; nothing "breaks" those laws to produce a thought. (Quantum randomness does not change this: random is not the same as free or intelligent.)

**Does it imply the former? No.**

1. **Level of description.** "Intelligence" describes the behaviour and **functional organization** of a system: perceiving, learning, reasoning and achieving goals in varied situations. That the low-level parts follow physical laws says nothing about what the whole system can do. Water molecules obey physics, yet waves and turbulence are real phenomena at a higher level.
2. **It proves too much.** The argument applies equally to **humans**. We are paradigm examples of intelligence, yet our atoms only do what physics dictates. If the argument were valid, humans would not be intelligent, which contradicts the very concept the argument relies on.
3. **For AI:** if intelligent human behaviour can arise from physical processes in the brain, then (by the physicalist and functionalist view) physical processes in a computer could produce it too. This is the basis of the "weak AI" claim that machines can act intelligently.

So the conclusion does not follow. Being governed by physical law is compatible with being intelligent.
