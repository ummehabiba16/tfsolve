**Is the latter statement true?** Essentially yes, as far as science knows: the atoms of animals, humans and computers obey the laws of physics, and their behaviour is determined by those laws (plus quantum randomness).

**Does it imply the former?** No.

1. **It proves too much.** If being governed by physical laws ruled out intelligence, then humans could not be intelligent either. But humans are the paradigm of intelligence, so the argument is a reductio ad absurdum of itself; the premise is true and the conclusion is false, so the inference is invalid.

2. **Levels of description.** Intelligence is a property of the behaviour and organisation of the whole system, not of its individual atoms. Atoms "do what the laws of physics tell them", but the brain as a system perceives, learns, reasons and plans; similarly a computer's transistors follow physics, but the program running on them can learn and solve problems. High-level properties (like a program playing chess well) can emerge from low-level rules without being written in them.

3. **Determinism vs intelligence.** Intelligence does not require "free" or law-breaking behaviour; it requires the right kind of information processing: acting rationally, adapting to new situations, learning from experience.

So the statement does not show that animals, humans or computers cannot be intelligent; whether a system is intelligent must be judged by what it can do.
