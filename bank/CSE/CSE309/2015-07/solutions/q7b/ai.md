Grammar: $F \to FBa \mid cDS \mid c$. It is left recursive ($F \to FBa$) and has a common prefix $c$ in $F \to cDS \mid c$.

**Route 1: eliminate left recursion first, then left-factor.**

Left recursion ($\alpha = Ba$, $\beta \in \{cDS, c\}$):

$$F \to cDS\,F' \mid c\,F'$$

$$F' \to Ba\,F' \mid \epsilon$$

Left factoring the common prefix $c$ of the two alternatives of $F$:

$$F \to c\,Z$$

$$Z \to DS\,F' \mid F'$$

$$F' \to Ba\,F' \mid \epsilon$$

**Route 2: left-factor first, then eliminate left recursion.**

Left factoring $F \to cDS \mid c$ (the alternative $FBa$ has no common prefix with them):

$$F \to FBa \mid c\,Y$$

$$Y \to DS \mid \epsilon$$

Left recursion ($\alpha = Ba$, $\beta = cY$):

$$F \to c\,Y\,F'$$

$$F' \to Ba\,F' \mid \epsilon$$

$$Y \to DS \mid \epsilon$$

**Comparison.** In Route 1, $F \to cZ$ with $Z \to DS\,F' \mid F'$; in Route 2, $F \to c\,Y\,F'$ with $Y \to DS \mid \epsilon$. Substituting $Y$: $Y F' = DS\,F' \mid F' = Z$. So the two grammars are the same grammar: $F$ derives $c$ followed by $(DS \mid \epsilon)$ followed by $F'$, with the same $F'$ ($F' \to Ba\,F' \mid \epsilon$), and they differ only in whether the optional part $(DS \mid \epsilon)\,F'$ is named once as $Z$ or built from $Y$ and $F'$. Both equal

$$F \to c\,DS\,F' \mid c\,F'$$

after folding the factored prefix, i.e. the grammar of Route 1 before left factoring. Hence **left factoring after removing left recursion yields the same grammar as removing left recursion after left factoring**; both generate for $F$ the language of the original grammar, $c\,(DS \mid \epsilon)\,(Ba)^*$.
